# A one-page summary of Nikita Bier's advice on making consumer apps go viral

## What it is
Anson Lin, who works in growth per his X bio, posts a two-page cheat sheet titled "Nikita Bier on how to make things go viral". Nikita Bier is a former Head of Product at X and founder of tbh (acquired by Meta) and Gas (acquired by Discord), per his X bio. The sheet is Lin's compilation of Bier's advice, grouped into themes (paraphrased here):
- **Test, don't launch:** replace "launch" with "test"; launch-then-iterate rarely works because people seldom give a product a second try; a repeatable testing process beats any single idea; if you need a nationwide launch to test, it's not a good test.
- **Start with a tiny, obsessive wedge:** narrow audiences like gamers, teens and hobbyists make the best beachhead; target a life inflection point (starting school, a first job, starting a company); be blatantly relevant to the first group.
- **Design for sharing:** people post to look smart, funny, attractive or rich, and products that get in the way share less; about half of a decision to try something comes from peers; aim for each session to bring in seven new people.
- **Buy density, not users:** ask how much the platforms will let you spend; people need to see marketing up to three times; the first 50 users are worth paying heavily for.
- **Study users like an anthropologist:** a dashboard can be one table (name, signup date, last active, sessions); message your best users directly; do things that don't scale.
- **Ship fast:** shipping speed is what matters in consumer software; keep a designer in the room; a product that offends someone may be one version from something special.
- **Distribution is the scarce skill:** AI coding shifts power to whoever can get distribution, and technical moats are mostly gone.
- **Grow on X and stay visible:** one subject, one unexpected insight a day for six months; captions on video; show up on the other apps people already use; if it can't be used while distracted, it won't become a habit.

## Implications
- **Mobile games and apps live or die on the first test:** soft-launching to a small, obsessive niche is the "test, don't launch" idea applied to games.
- **Build sharing into the core loop:** shareable moments should make the player look good (skill, humour, rarity), not just advertise the app.
- **A one-table dashboard is enough early on:** last-active and session counts per user, plus direct conversations with the best users, beat elaborate analytics before product-market fit.
- **Experiment:** pick one narrow community for a prototype, run a small paid test to just that group with one share-worthy moment designed in, and track sessions per user and invites per session against a broad-audience control.
